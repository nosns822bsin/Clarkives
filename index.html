No. 01 — The Archive

This Is Not
A New Idea.

Clarkives started in 2026 with the frustration that fashionable clothing is rarely sustainable and sustainable clothing is rarely fashionable. We had to change this. Every piece is inspired by the timelessness of vintage menswear: made again, but the right way.

Other brands start with clothing first, then sustainability. We don't greenwash. Our target is for every Clarkives piece to as sustainable as possible, whilst delivering a premium feel at prices that protect the planet and your wallet.

Each garment carries a handwritten number stitched inside. It's a record of what the fabric used to be, where it came from, and which drop it belongs to — an archive you can actually wear.

No. 02 — The Materials

What "Nothing New" Actually Means

Six commitments that shape every drop, not just the marketing around it.

01

Deadstock First

Every drop starts with offcut and deadstock fabric sourced from mills across the UK and EU — material that would otherwise be incinerated or landfilled, not grown or drilled for.

02

Ocean-Bound rPET

Shells and trims use recycled polyester spun from ocean-bound plastic, collected from coastal communities before it ever reaches the sea.

03

Low-Impact Dyeing

Where fabric needs colour, we use plant-based and low-impact dyes through a closed-loop water system, cutting run-off compared with conventional synthetic dyeing.

04

Repair, Don't Replace

Bring any Clarkives piece back to the studio for free first-repair. Patches and spare parts are cut from our own offcuts, so the fix stays true to the archive.

05

Traceable Supply

Organic cotton is GOTS-certified, cutting is done in-house in East London, and our partner units are aligned with Fair Wear Foundation standards.

06

Take-Back Scheme

Send worn-out Clarkives pieces back to us for store credit. Garments are re-graded into future drops or broken down and recycled — nothing goes to waste twice.

No. 04 — The Process

How a Drop Gets Archived

  1. 01

    Source

    Deadstock and offcut fabric is collected from mills across the UK and EU on a quarterly buying run.

  2. 02

    Sort & Grade

    Every metre is logged, graded by fibre and colour, and matched to a pattern before it's approved for cutting.

  3. 03

    Cut & Construct

    Small batches are cut in our London studio using zero-waste marker layouts to minimise fabric loss.

  4. 04

    Number & Ship

    Each piece gets a catalogue number stitched inside, then ships in recycled kraft mailers, plastic-free.
